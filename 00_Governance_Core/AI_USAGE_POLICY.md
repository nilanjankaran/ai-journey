# AI Usage Policy

**Policy owner:** AI Governance / Enterprise Architecture
**Approved by:** Nilanjan(Neil) Karan
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Draft for organisational review  
**Effective date:** 8th Oct 2026  
**Review date:** 8th Oct 2026  

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes consistent, responsible, secure, and accountable use of artificial intelligence across the organisation. It is technology- and vendor-neutral and applies equally to commercial AI assistants, embedded copilots, foundation models, generative AI, machine-learning services, autonomous or semi-autonomous agents, and future AI capabilities.

The policy is designed so that changing a model or platform does not reduce the required governance, evidence, privacy, security, or human-accountability standard.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- employees, contractors, consultants, suppliers, and other authorised users;
- all organisational devices, accounts, data, systems, and business processes;
- free, paid, trial, consumer, enterprise, hosted, open-source, and internally developed AI services;
- AI used for text, code, images, audio, video, analytics, search, summarisation, decision support, automation, agents, or system actions;
- AI capabilities embedded in productivity suites, business applications, development tools, browsers, APIs, and connected platforms.

It applies whether AI is used directly through a user interface or indirectly through an integration, workflow, plug-in, connector, application, or agent.

## 3. Policy principles

1. **Human accountability:** AI may support work, but accountable people retain ownership of decisions, approvals, communications, and outcomes.
2. **Lawful and authorised use:** AI use must comply with law, contract, intellectual-property obligations, records requirements, and organisational policy.
3. **Proportionate risk management:** oversight increases with impact, autonomy, data sensitivity, scale, affected population, and difficulty of reversal.
4. **Privacy and data minimisation:** use only the minimum authorised data needed for a legitimate purpose.
5. **Security by design:** identity, access, data protection, secure configuration, monitoring, and incident controls must be applied throughout the lifecycle.
6. **Transparency:** people must not be materially misled about AI use, AI-generated content, or the basis of consequential decisions.
7. **Fairness and accessibility:** assess and mitigate the risk of harmful, discriminatory, systematically unequal, or inaccessible outcomes.
8. **Evidence and reliability:** validate important outputs using authoritative sources, testing, and human review.
9. **Contestability and remedy:** consequential use must provide an appropriate path for review, correction, escalation, or appeal.
10. **Lifecycle governance:** registration, design, testing, deployment, monitoring, change, incident management, and retirement must be governed.

## 4. Approved platforms and accounts

- Use only AI services, models, integrations, plug-ins, and accounts approved for the intended data classification and use case.
- Organisational work must be performed through authorised organisational accounts where available.
- Consumer or personal accounts must not be used for confidential, personal, regulated, privileged, commercially sensitive, security-sensitive, or non-public organisational information.
- Enabling model training, persistent memory, public sharing, third-party plug-ins, external connectors, or data retention beyond approved settings requires authorisation.
- Approval of a platform does not automatically approve every use case, data type, model, plug-in, connector, or autonomous action.

## 5. Data handling

Users must:

- classify information before providing it to an AI service;
- follow least privilege, need-to-know, purpose limitation, and data-minimisation requirements;
- remove or mask personal information, secrets, credentials, privileged material, and unnecessary identifiers where practical;
- confirm where prompts, files, outputs, logs, embeddings, fine-tuning data, and telemetry are stored, processed, retained, and accessible when required by policy;
- ensure retrieval and generated responses honour source-system permissions;
- use approved secure methods for transferring data;
- comply with data residency, retention, legal hold, deletion, and records obligations.

Do not enter authentication secrets, private keys, access tokens, passwords, production credentials, or encryption keys into an AI system.

## 6. Acceptable use

Subject to platform and data approval, acceptable uses may include:

- drafting, summarising, translating, classifying, and formatting content;
- brainstorming, research assistance, and option generation;
- coding assistance, test creation, documentation, and controlled analysis;
- retrieval and synthesis of approved knowledge with citations;
- low-risk productivity support where a competent person reviews the output;
- decision support where evidence, limitations, and accountable human judgement remain explicit.

All outputs must be reviewed at a level proportionate to their impact before use or distribution.

## 7. Restricted and prohibited use

The following uses are prohibited unless an explicit lawful exception and formal approval exist, and some may remain prohibited in all circumstances:

- unlawful, deceptive, manipulative, discriminatory, harassing, or harmful activity;
- bypassing security controls, approvals, access restrictions, segregation of duties, monitoring, or audit requirements;
- unauthorised surveillance, profiling, re-identification, or inference of sensitive personal characteristics;
- autonomous employment, disciplinary, legal, financial, safety, healthcare, security, regulatory, or customer-impacting decisions without approved human oversight;
- generating or distributing knowingly false, misleading, defamatory, fraudulent, or impersonated content;
- exposing confidential, privileged, personal, regulated, or security-sensitive information to an unapproved service or recipient;
- using unverified AI output as the sole basis for a consequential decision;
- creating malware, exploiting systems, or facilitating unauthorised access;
- infringing copyright, licence terms, confidentiality, moral rights, trademarks, patents, or other intellectual-property obligations;
- deploying an AI agent with uncontrolled permissions, unbounded objectives, or authority to perform irreversible actions.

## 8. Risk classification and governance

Every material AI use case must be registered and assessed before production or broad organisational adoption. Assessment must consider:

- impact on people, rights, finances, safety, customers, operations, and reputation;
- autonomy and ability to act in other systems;
- data sensitivity and privacy;
- fairness, accessibility, and affected groups;
- security threats, including prompt injection, data exfiltration, malicious content, and supply-chain risk;
- reliability, explainability, human dependence, and reversibility;
- legal, regulatory, contractual, records, and intellectual-property obligations;
- model, vendor, integration, and concentration risk.

Use the organisation's approved risk-tiering method. Higher-risk use requires stronger evidence, specialist review, formal approval, human oversight, monitoring, and documented residual-risk acceptance. Prohibited use must not proceed.

## 9. Human oversight

For consequential use cases:

- name the accountable business owner;
- define the AI's role and the human decision authority;
- ensure reviewers have sufficient competence, context, time, and ability to challenge the output;
- avoid automation bias and rubber-stamping;
- provide an escalation, correction, and override mechanism;
- prevent the system from taking high-impact or irreversible action without explicit confirmation and appropriate authorisation.

AI systems must not be described as accountable, responsible, approving, or owning a decision.

## 10. Accuracy, grounding, and content review

Users must treat AI output as potentially incomplete, incorrect, outdated, biased, insecure, or fabricated.

Before relying on material output:

- verify factual claims against authoritative and current sources;
- validate quotations, references, names, dates, calculations, code, and links;
- require citations or traceability where the use case depends on evidence;
- distinguish source facts from model inference and user assumptions;
- test output against defined acceptance criteria;
- obtain qualified review for legal, financial, safety, security, privacy, regulatory, medical, employment, or public claims.

AI-generated code must undergo the same security review, testing, software-composition analysis, change control, and deployment approval as human-authored code.

## 11. Transparency and disclosure

Disclose AI involvement when required by law, policy, contract, professional obligation, or when omission could materially mislead the audience.

For material decisions or published content, retain enough information to explain:

- the purpose of the AI use;
- the model or service used, where required;
- the data and sources relied upon;
- the human review performed;
- known limitations and material uncertainty;
- the accountable owner and approval path.

Do not falsely represent AI-generated content as independently verified human work.

## 12. Fairness, inclusion, and accessibility

Before deployment to people-facing or decision-influencing processes:

- identify affected users and potentially disadvantaged groups;
- test for materially different error rates or outcomes where appropriate and lawful;
- assess whether prompts, training data, retrieval sources, features, or feedback loops encode harmful bias;
- provide accessible alternatives and reasonable human review channels;
- monitor complaints, overrides, and adverse outcomes;
- document mitigations and remaining limitations.

Protected or sensitive attributes and their proxies must not be used unless lawful, necessary, approved, and appropriately controlled.

## 13. Security requirements

AI solutions must apply relevant security controls, including:

- strong identity, authentication, authorisation, and least privilege;
- separation of development, test, and production environments;
- secret management and secure API usage;
- input, output, and tool-call validation;
- protection against prompt injection, indirect instruction attacks, data poisoning, insecure plug-ins, and data leakage;
- allow-listed tools, destinations, and actions for agents;
- logging, monitoring, anomaly detection, rate and cost limits, and emergency disablement;
- vulnerability, dependency, and supply-chain management;
- security testing before deployment and after significant change.

Retrieved content, tool output, user-supplied files, and external webpages must be treated as potentially hostile. They must not override system policy or cause unauthorised actions.

## 14. Agents and automated actions

AI agents require additional governance because they can plan, call tools, and act across systems.

An agent must have:

- a bounded purpose, approved tools, defined data access, and maximum authority;
- least-privilege credentials that are separate from personal administrator credentials;
- explicit confirmation for consequential, external, destructive, irreversible, financial, legal, production, or security-sensitive actions;
- limits for time, spend, volume, recursion, and retries;
- deterministic controls outside the model for critical permissions and prohibited actions;
- complete action logging and traceability;
- safe failure, rollback where feasible, monitoring, and a kill switch;
- an accountable product or business owner and operational support model.

Agents must not grant themselves permissions, change their governing instructions, conceal actions, or expand their objective without approval.

## 15. Intellectual property and third-party content

Users must:

- respect copyright, licences, confidentiality, trademarks, patents, and contractual restrictions;
- verify rights to upload, transform, retrieve, reproduce, or publish source material;
- check generated output for unauthorised reproduction and attribution requirements;
- comply with approved software licences before using AI-generated code or dependencies;
- protect organisational intellectual property and trade secrets;
- involve legal or procurement specialists where ownership, indemnity, licensing, or training rights are unclear.

## 16. Records, logging, and auditability

AI-related records must be retained according to the applicable records schedule and risk tier. Depending on the use case, records may include:

- intake and approval;
- risk assessment and design decisions;
- model, prompt, configuration, data-source, and tool versions;
- evaluation results and release evidence;
- material prompts, outputs, citations, human changes, and approvals;
- action logs, incidents, complaints, overrides, monitoring, and review outcomes.

Logging must be proportionate and must not itself create unnecessary privacy or security exposure.

## 17. Evaluation and monitoring

Before production, define measurable acceptance criteria relevant to the use case, such as factual accuracy, groundedness, task completion, safety, security, fairness, latency, resilience, accessibility, and cost.

Production systems must be monitored for:

- quality deterioration and model or data drift;
- unsupported or harmful output;
- security events and policy violations;
- abnormal access, tool use, spend, or volume;
- human overrides, complaints, and adverse outcomes;
- vendor, model, integration, and knowledge-source changes.

Material changes require reassessment and, where applicable, reapproval.

## 18. Incident management

Suspected AI incidents must be reported through the approved security, privacy, operational-risk, legal, safety, or service-management process as appropriate.

Users must preserve relevant evidence and must not conceal, delete, or independently remediate records where doing so could compromise an investigation. The organisation must be able to suspend the affected model, integration, agent, data source, or use case while impact is assessed.

## 19. Roles and responsibilities

- **Business owner:** accountable for purpose, value, lawful use, risk acceptance, and in-life outcomes.
- **Users:** follow this policy, protect data, validate outputs, and report issues.
- **Enterprise Architecture / Architecture Review Board:** assesses alignment with principles, reference architecture, standards, integration, and lifecycle design.
- **AI governance:** maintains policy, use-case register, risk method, governance gates, and oversight reporting.
- **Security:** reviews threats, controls, testing, access, monitoring, and incidents.
- **Privacy and data governance:** reviews lawful processing, personal information, quality, lineage, retention, and access.
- **Legal, compliance, records, procurement, and risk:** advise and approve within their mandates.
- **Technology or product owner:** manages service operation, evaluation, change, support, vendor dependencies, and retirement.

Role names must be mapped to the organisation's actual accountabilities before this policy is approved.

## 20. Exceptions

Exceptions must be documented, time-bound, risk-assessed, and approved by the authorised owner before use. The exception must record:

- policy requirement affected;
- business justification;
- scope and duration;
- compensating controls;
- residual risk and accountable risk acceptor;
- review and expiry date.

An exception cannot authorise unlawful or expressly prohibited activity.

## 21. Compliance and enforcement

Failure to comply may result in removal of access, suspension of the use case, investigation, contractual action, or disciplinary action in accordance with applicable organisational processes. Enforcement must be fair, proportionate, and consistent.

## 22. Review and maintenance

This policy must be reviewed periodically and when there is a material change in law, regulation, organisational risk appetite, approved platforms, model capability, security threat, or operating model.

Platform-specific standards may supplement this policy but must not weaken it.

## Appendix A: Minimum user checklist

Before using AI for organisational work, confirm:

- [ ] The platform, account, model, plug-ins, and connectors are approved.
- [ ] The use case is permitted and registered if required.
- [ ] The information is classified and permitted for this service.
- [ ] Only the minimum necessary data is included.
- [ ] No secrets or unnecessary personal information are exposed.
- [ ] Important outputs will be verified against authoritative sources.
- [ ] A competent person will review the output before consequential use.
- [ ] Required disclosure, records, citation, and approval obligations are understood.
- [ ] Any system action is bounded, authorised, logged, and reversible where feasible.
- [ ] Suspected incidents can be reported through an approved path.

## Appendix B: Minimum use-case registration fields

- Use-case name and identifier
- Business purpose and intended benefit
- Accountable business owner
- Users and affected parties
- AI service, model, deployment, integrations, tools, and agents
- Data sources, classifications, residency, and retention
- Decision influence and level of autonomy
- Risk tier and assessment rationale
- Human oversight and contestability
- Evaluation criteria and results
- Security, privacy, legal, data, records, and architecture reviews
- Monitoring, incident, change, support, and retirement arrangements
- Approval status, conditions, exceptions, and review date

## Appendix C: External alignment

This policy is intended to be adaptable to recognised risk-management approaches, including the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and its Generative AI Profile. Organisational legal and compliance teams must determine which laws, regulations, standards, and contractual requirements apply in each jurisdiction and use case.

## Appendix D: Internal adoption notes

Before approval, the organisation should:

1. Replace bracketed placeholders and map roles to actual accountable bodies.
2. Define approved AI platforms by data classification and capability.
3. Link the AI use-case register, risk-tiering method, incident process, records schedule, architecture review, security standard, and privacy assessment.
4. Define which activities require disclosure and human confirmation.
5. Establish review cadence, exception authority, and enforcement process.
6. Obtain legal, privacy, security, data-governance, records, procurement, risk, HR, and employee-representative review as applicable.
